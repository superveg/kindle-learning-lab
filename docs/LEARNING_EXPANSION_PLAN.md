# Learning expansion plan — six levels beyond mathematics

Status: planning specification; new subject modules are not implemented. Maintain this plan in place as designs become implemented or evidence changes. This is not a change log or a claim of educational effectiveness.

## Confirmed scope and delivery order

The owner approved exploring five additional subjects in this order: **Logic → Chinese → English → Everyday Science → Stories and Social Situations**. First document the complete outline and details; implement separately in later work. The immediate learner is three years old. Keep six freely selectable levels, with a broad planning envelope of roughly ages 3–8/9. Levels are task structures, not age norms, grades, diagnoses or locks. Reading level may differ between Chinese and English, and logic must not depend on reading ability.

Existing mathematics stays available. The original Hub's shape, letter and story samples are diagnostics/prototypes, not completed versions of these curricula. The new plan proposes five subject modules, thirty subject/level combinations and three initial activity families per combination (ninety template specifications, not ninety fixed questions). This is a staged target; do not publish placeholders as working activities.

## Common child experience

1. Choose subject → choose one of six available levels → choose one of the three activity families at that level. Show the same numbered stair badge used by math, a short capability description and a pictured example of the next level. Remembering the last selection may be added with a separate, failure-tolerant key; never overwrite math progress.
2. Levels 1–2 use pictures and two or three choices. A pictured sample must explain the action without requiring the child to read. Chinese/English word meanings may need a grown-up narrator: label that mode honestly, rather than implying independent vocabulary learning from silent pictures.
3. Tap to choose, select an object then its destination, or tap cards in order. Dragging, typing, handwriting, microphone input and audio are not prerequisites. Provide Undo for constructions/order tasks. Do not make diagrams unexplained buttons.
4. The last required action establishes completion automatically. In an ordering task, each tap appends one card; allow any prefix, evaluate at the final card, and allow Undo after an incorrect full sequence. Do not require every prefix to match a hidden canonical sequence. Accept all valid orders under the stated constraints.
5. Completion is a natural pause. Another question remains at the same subject/activity/level, without a fixed cap; Back remains available. No timer, automatic advancement, forced level-up or speed rewards. Open exploration uses a clearly labeled Finish/Another story action rather than a correctness Submit button.
6. Cues and optional hints describe the current action. Clear stale error feedback when a new valid action begins. Completion replaces unfinished instructions with a result and manual continuation. A hint cannot be a gate to the answer.
7. High-contrast monochrome art, large touch targets, calm static scenes and user-triggered changes. Shape, size, texture and position convey distinctions; color alone never determines an answer. Scattered or rotated art changes between questions, never while solving.
8. Children may use different levels across subjects. Store played marks, not inferred mastery. Suggestions to try a harder example are optional; do not assess ability from a handful of taps.

## Six-level design contract

| Level | Structural demand | Presentation target | Scaffolding |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 — Notice | Recognize one visible feature or familiar association | 2–3 large choices; no independent reading | Pictured target and one-action example |
| 2 — Connect | Relate two things; follow one rule or a short sequence | 3–4 choices; 2–3 ordered cards | Rule stays visible; adult narration available |
| 3 — Combine | Combine two attributes, components or linked facts | 3–5 choices; up to 4 cards | Visible components and optional current-step hint |
| 4 — Use clues | Use two explicit clues or a short linked text | 3–4 meaningful alternatives | Relevant clue remains visible; no memory gate |
| 5 — Reason | Connect several clues, events or observations | Two stages; small passage/table, not a crowded screen | Step labels, Undo and evidence highlighting |
| 6 — Explain | Infer, compare explanations or test a rule | Short scenario with 3–4 reason/evidence cards | Accept multiple defensible responses where appropriate |

These counts are initial layout limits to test, not developmental milestones. Higher levels increase relations, inference and reduced scaffolding; larger option banks or longer text alone do not establish a higher level.

## 1. Logic Detective / 逻辑小侦探

Purpose: observation, relationships, sequencing, deduction and checking rules without arithmetic or reading prerequisites. Share existing spatial widgets where useful, but give the task a distinct objective; do not simply relabel math sorting/route questions.

### Six-level activity outline

| Level | Three activity families | Representative task and interaction | Difficulty boundary |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | Exact match; one changed detail; match a partial picture | Find the train with the same window/roof as the target. Tap one of three complete pictures. | One relevant feature; no tiny differences or rotation requirement |
| 2 | Functional pairs; one-rule grouping; everyday order | Pair key with lock; put socks on before shoes; choose objects matching a displayed texture. Select item then destination, or tap 2–3 cards in order. | One explicit relationship; enough context to avoid arbitrary “goes together” answers |
| 3 | Two-feature rule; picture analogy; sequence completion | Pick the striped object with a round outline; map a filled circle to outlined circle, then apply the pictured transformation to a square. | Two attributes or one demonstrated transformation; nonnumeric patterns |
| 4 | Eliminate by two clues; before/after constraints; constrained route | “Has wheels” and “not the vehicle with a roof,” shown as icons, identify one vehicle. Arrange three events with two shown constraints. | Two clues needed; no answer from one incidental picture cue |
| 5 | Small assignment puzzle; dependency chain; repair an arrangement | Assign three animals to three homes using visible exclusion clues; arrange actions where one needs another first. Tap swaps or ordered cards, then evaluate the final arrangement. | 3 entities and 2–3 relationships; more than one reasoning step |
| 6 | Rule/counterexample; sufficient evidence; multiple valid plans | “Every striped animal is in this pen”: find the pictured exception. Decide which clue would distinguish two remaining suspects. | Explain via a reason card; allow “not enough information” and all valid plans |

### Content and generation details

- Three art packs to start: vehicles, toy animals and household objects. Vehicles can help entry engagement, but alternate packs so success transfers beyond a favorite theme.
- Data fields: objects with explicit feature values; stated rules/constraints; answer mode; valid solution set; clue icons; explanation; task signature. Keep answer-bearing attributes visually distinguishable in black and white.
- Randomize feature combinations, scene assignments, which feature changes, clue order and answer position. A new illustration of the same logic relation does not count as a new underlying task.
- For unique-answer deduction, enumerate all assignments and reject tasks with zero or multiple solutions. For open construction, record the predicate and accept every arrangement satisfying it. Before/after cards may have several valid total orders.
- A wrong answer highlights the specific unmet visible rule after the child chooses, without changing the puzzle. At level 6, insufficient information is a legitimate authored answer; never force a guess.
- Avoid bare odd-one-out sets with several plausible distinctions. Supply a pictured criterion, or author several accepted explanations.

Acceptance: nonreaders can identify the intended action in levels 1–2 with a single demonstration; solver and validator agree; Undo restores placement and guidance; alternative valid orders pass; answer does not depend on color or tiny details.

## 2. Chinese Story House / 中文故事屋

Purpose: meaning, vocabulary, useful print, sentences and comprehension. Spoken vocabulary and character recognition are separate strands. Picture matching is not proof of reading, and silent text is not proof of oral language learning.

Content convention: first implementation uses a consistent Simplified Chinese child-content pack with Mandarin adult narration; Traditional Chinese is a separate future equivalent pack, not mixed distractors. This script choice is a reversible proposal, not a confirmed family preference. No compulsory pinyin or stroke tracing in the first release.

### Six-level activity outline

| Level | Three activity families | Representative task and interaction | Difficulty boundary |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | Familiar picture words; actions in pictures; story-picture association | A grown-up says “杯子”; choose its picture. Match eating/running/sleeping scenes; choose what a pictured rabbit is using. | Everyday meanings with adult narration; no independent character requirement |
| 2 | Character-to-picture; useful labels; pictured event order | Match 山、水、门 to pictures; find the 出口 label in a toy scene; order washing/drying hands. | Small introduced word set; printed target remains visible |
| 3 | Word construction; short phrase match; subject/action relation | Combine 火 + 车 into 火车; match 大大的杯子 to a clear size comparison; distinguish “猫追狗” from “狗追猫.” | Known components and changed relationships, not an unexplained new character bank |
| 4 | Short-sentence meaning; sentence ordering; missing meaningful word | “小兔在桌子下面。” Choose the pictured position. Tap chunks “小兔 / 吃 / 胡萝卜” in a valid order; complete a sentence from a small word bank. | One sentence, introduced vocabulary; equivalent natural wordings accepted |
| 5 | Short narrative sequence; stated cause; pronoun/reference | In a 3–4 sentence original story, find who “他” refers to, order events, or select the stated reason a character went inside. | Connect information across sentences; passage always available |
| 6 | Inference with evidence; coherent ending; revise a sentence | Infer why a character brought an umbrella from explicit weather/action clues; choose a consistent ending and its evidence card; repair an unclear referent. | Text-supported reasoning, not guessing author intent or memorized idioms |

### Content and generation details

- Start vocabulary domains: family, body, food, vehicles, animals, home, actions and positions. Introduce a small visible set before practice, then reuse it in different combinations. The exact vocabulary inventory is an authoring task, not an age quota.
- Suggested content budgets: level 1–2 cards have 1–2 characters/one word where needed; level 3 phrases roughly 2–6 characters; level 4 one short sentence; level 5 roughly 30–70 characters; level 6 roughly 60–120 characters. Adjust for actual layout and comprehension, not a fixed reading-speed target.
- Author reviewed banks for vocabulary, accepted synonyms, sentence chunks and original stories. Randomize participants/objects only within checked grammatical and meaningful combinations. Do not freely stitch nouns and verbs into nonsense.
- Distractors target meaning: reversed agent, changed action, or changed position. Do not make the correct option identifiable because it is longer, cleaner, or the only grammatical sentence.
- Level 1–2 companion mode includes a folded narration line and optional conversation prompt; target picture should not reveal the answer merely by duplication unless visual matching is the stated objective.
- Sentence validators use accepted orders or grammatical constraints; do not reject another natural order. Reference tasks require one clearly introduced antecedent. Distinguish “the text says” from “a possible explanation.”
- Test every character on Kindle. If the system font lacks Chinese glyphs, provide reviewed text-image cards for essential targets rather than making the whole interface unreadable. Keep text accessible in the DOM; test asset weight before expanding the inventory.

Acceptance: all words/characters render; picture-supported and independent-reading modes are distinguished; language and accepted alternatives receive human editorial review; story answers are justified by the text; no compulsory handwriting, keyboard or audio.

## 3. English Explorer / 英语探险

Purpose: meaningful vocabulary, print awareness, letter recognition, word building and reading comprehension. A silent word-picture game is not a full phonics or speaking course. This module can launch with adult narration and visual practice while sound support is separately evaluated.

### Six-level activity outline

| Level | Three activity families | Representative task and interaction | Difficulty boundary |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | Familiar picture vocabulary; actions; simple picture instructions | Grown-up says “cup”; child chooses a picture. Match a sleeping animal; follow a pictured “put in” instruction. | Meaning with narration; not unassisted reading |
| 2 | Upper/lowercase pairs; familiar word labels; category/meaning pairs | Pair A/a, B/b; match an introduced “cat” card to its picture; identify food versus vehicles under a shown label/example. | Letter form and introduced words; avoid treating case pairing as sound knowledge |
| 3 | Word construction; initial-letter association; phrase meaning | Build “cat” from introduced letter tiles with a pictured/printed model; associate known “bus” with b; match “a big dog.” | Without sound, label these spelling/print tasks; no claim that first-letter matching teaches a phoneme |
| 4 | Sentence meaning; sentence ordering; action/location contrast | Match “The cat is under the chair”; arrange “The dog / runs”; choose “in” or “on” with a clear scene. | One short sentence with taught vocabulary; all grammatical alternatives considered |
| 5 | Short story facts; reference; linked instructions | Read 3–5 short sentences; identify who “she” refers to, what happened next or follow two linked instructions. | Connect sentences; text remains visible, no memory-only test |
| 6 | Inference/evidence; consistent ending; edit for meaning | Choose an ending supported by a short passage and a reason card; revise a sentence with an ambiguous pronoun or mismatched detail. | Comprehension and explanation; not an arbitrary jump to long rare vocabulary |

### Content and generation details

- Reuse familiar concepts across Chinese and English, but keep separate lexical inventories and progress; do not require translation or assume equal proficiency.
- Start with home, family, food, vehicles, animals, actions and spatial words. Use consistent American English spelling and narration notes. Limit unsupported new words per task; offer a visible familiar-word reference without giving the complete answer.
- Proposed budgets: levels 1–2 one word per card; level 3 2–4-word phrases; level 4 4–8-word sentences; level 5 roughly 20–45-word passages; level 6 roughly 40–80 words. These are authoring/layout targets, not grade standards.
- For word tiles, supply enough copies of repeated letters and accept identical tiles interchangeably. A pictured model makes it copying/word construction, not decoding; practice labels must reflect that.
- Reviewed sentence templates enforce number, article, verb and pronoun consistency. Store accepted word orders; reject unnatural generated sentences before display. Story distractors must be meaningful and evidence-linked.
- Optional future audio strand: adult-initiated replay, sound discrimination, oral blending and systematic letter/sound correspondences using reviewed American English recordings. First test supported audio formats, tap/replay/stop, speaker availability and loaded-page behavior on the actual Kindle. If unavailable, keep sound tasks out of the independent child menu; provide clearly labeled adult-led prompts. No autoplay, microphone scoring or text-to-speech dependency.
- Do not introduce arbitrary CVC banks without the sounds having been taught. A future phonics sequence needs its own reviewed progression; these six visual levels do not establish it.

Acceptance: visual and listening tasks are accurately labeled; all repeated-letter and alternate-order cases work; distractors test intended meaning; no silent task masquerades as pronunciation/phonemic-awareness assessment.

## 4. Everyday Science / 生活科学

Purpose: observe, compare, predict, inspect evidence and revise explanations. Unlike abstract logic, answers rely on pictured or supplied observations about the physical/natural world. The page supplements real exploration rather than pretending a tap is a physical experiment.

### Six-level activity outline

| Level | Three activity families | Representative task and interaction | Difficulty boundary |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | Observable parts; familiar use; scene-detail match | Find the leaf on a plant; match a cup with a drinking scene; locate the pictured raindrops. | Direct observation, not trivia or assumed background knowledge |
| 2 | Observable category; weather preparation; growth order | Sort shown textures; choose rain boots for a wet puddle scene; order seed → sprout → plant. | One supplied property or clear event chain |
| 3 | Cause/prediction; needs in context; compare observations | A tilted toy ramp is shown: predict the direction a pictured ball rolls; compare two plant records under stated conditions. | One variable/relationship; explanation rests on shown information |
| 4 | Observation versus prediction; record reading; testable question | Identify “the leaf is wet” as observation versus “it may rain”; read icon records; choose a question a pictured investigation can test. | Two linked pieces of evidence; introduces limits of a claim |
| 5 | Fair comparison; evidence/conclusion; revise prediction | Two seedling setups differ in water and light: identify why water alone cannot explain the difference. Select a better comparison; compare expected and observed outcomes. | One-variable comparison and multi-record reasoning |
| 6 | Competing explanations; uncertainty; next investigation | Evidence fits both shade and water explanations: select “not enough evidence” and an experiment changing only one factor. | Distinguish support from proof; choose evidence that separates explanations |

### Content and generation details

- Initial strands: plants, animal observations, weather, everyday materials and simple motion. Each factual relationship has a source note or a clearly specified fictional observation dataset.
- Generate from vetted scenario families and internal evidence records. Randomization changes conditions and observations within the family; explanation and accepted conclusions must change consistently.
- Predictions are hypotheses, not mistakes to punish. Flow: choose prediction → explicitly choose Observe → show authored result → compare/explain. Observe is a labeled experiment action, not a hidden prerequisite tap. No live sensor or real-time simulation is implied.
- Avoid universal shortcuts: “all metal sinks,” “all birds fly,” “plants only need water,” or habitat/diet pairs with many plausible answers. Specify the object/species/conditions and include counterexamples where appropriate.
- Living/nonliving classifications should avoid ambiguous examples in early tasks; dead leaves and dormant seeds need discussion rather than an unexplained red cross.
- Optional off-screen activity cards use safe materials with a grown-up; no device-dependent timing or mandatory uploads. The page must remain usable without performing the home experiment.
- Nature pictures must be recognizable in monochrome. Do not depend on season color, taste, sound or texture that a screen cannot supply unless observations are explicitly provided.

Acceptance: every claim is traceable; generated evidence supports the accepted conclusion; fair-test conditions actually isolate the variable; uncertainty answers are supported; changing a prediction never generates punitive scoring.

## 5. Stories and Social Situations / 故事与社会情境

Purpose: sequence, perspective, expressing needs and exploring helpful responses. This is not obedience training, an emotion diagnosis or a quiz with one correct way to feel. Unlike language comprehension, choices here may explore several reasonable paths.

### Six-level activity outline

| Level | Three activity families | Representative task and interaction | Difficulty boundary |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | Story-detail recognition; possible feeling; comfort choice | A rabbit loses a toy: choose a possible feeling or comfort action. Show context, not a face-only “emotion detector.” | One event with adult narration; multiple feelings may fit |
| 2 | Event order; ask for help; need/action association | Order a lost-toy story; choose asking an adult or looking with a friend; connect tired/hungry scenes with plausible needs. | Simple cause/response; feelings are suggestions, not labels forced on the child |
| 3 | Two perspectives; expressing a boundary; repair | Two animals want one toy. Choose “I am using it” or ask for a turn; an animal knocks blocks over and chooses a way to help rebuild. | Both viewpoints visible; helpful choices can differ |
| 4 | Consequence branches; feeling versus action; choose a plan | Explore what might happen after grabbing, asking or waiting. Feeling angry is acceptable; hurting someone prompts a safer action. | Compare consequences without punishment, shaming or forced affection |
| 5 | Conflict with constraints; intention/effect; help-seeking plan | One child needs quiet and another wants music. Explore location/time options; distinguish an accident from its effect; choose a safe help-seeking sequence. | Several needs or steps; more than one workable plan |
| 6 | Ambiguous intent; missing information; tradeoffs | A friend did not answer: consider several explanations, choose what to ask, and compare two reasonable resolutions. | Uncertainty and perspective; do not teach mind-reading or automatic blame |

### Content and generation details

- Original fictional vignettes: play, turn-taking, transitions, frustration, mistakes, boundaries and asking for help. Do not import a real classmate's name or private family incident into the content bank.
- Separate answer modes: objective story-detail question; multiple acceptable helpful responses; open exploration. Only the first uses ordinary correct/retry feedback. Helpful branches show a neutral explanation/consequence; open stories offer Another path/Finish with no correctness score.
- A potentially harmful action may be pictured as a brief alternative with a calm explanation and an immediately available safer choice. Do not depict distress as a reward animation or escalate a scenario to keep the child tapping.
- Use explicit context for feelings; several feeling cards may be valid. Optional “another feeling/not sure” supports ambiguity. Do not claim facial expression reveals an inner state with certainty.
- Branches end after 2–4 child choices with a natural pause. All reachable branches have a defined ending, Back and a clear way to explore another path. Randomization selects reviewed stories/roles, not automatically generated moral judgments.
- Questions about boundaries preserve the option to refuse physical affection and seek a trusted adult. Agreement, waiting or sharing is not automatically the required answer in every scenario.
- Adult guide offers a question such as “What else might help?” No required recording of personal emotions, names, speech or behavior profiles.

Acceptance: all branches terminate; reasonable alternatives are acknowledged; feeling choices are not punished; objective recall is separated from social judgment; boundaries and help-seeking remain available.

## Shared content and implementation architecture

Proposed structure: keep `math.html` stable; add `logic.html`, `chinese.html`, `english.html`, `science.html`, `stories.html` one at a time. These are proposed paths, not existing live links. Each deployed page contains the assets/data needed for its session; no question-time network dependency. Use authoring sources plus a simple build script only if duplication warrants it; generated pages stay inspectable static HTML with conservative JavaScript. Avoid a new framework or a mandatory bundler for the site.

A common authored-task contract should contain:

- stable subject/family/level/task IDs, content language and answer mode;
- learning objective, prerequisite words/concepts, visible prompt and pictured clue;
- scene data and answer controls; accepted solutions/predicate, reason and distractor rationale;
- phase-specific cues/hints, Undo state, completion summary and adult narration;
- randomization constraints, canonical signature and fact/editorial source where needed.

Runtime generators select and combine reviewed banks. No live AI-generated child content. Reuse math's balanced shuffled variants and recent-eight signature approach where the eligible bank permits it; finite stories repeat gracefully. Signatures ignore layout/art/option order and represent the actual task. Keep a generated task stable through hint, retry and Undo.

Navigation, feedback and choice widgets can be shared in source, but subject-specific validators stay explicit. Do not refactor the entire math app merely to add the first subject. Keep separate storage namespaces; failures fall back to a working session. Proposed marks record played subject/family/level only. No accounts, cross-device sync, adaptive mastery, required saved question state or offline reopening promise.

## Implementation sequence and review gates

Complete one subject before moving to the next; within it, ship small usable slices. This sequence is a plan, not an instruction to implement during the planning task.

| Step | Concrete work | Exit condition before the next step |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 0 — Plan | Review this outline, select the first art/content packs and preserve existing product rules | This document and discovery links exist; proposed versus implemented status is explicit |
| 1 — Logic | Implement entry and 3 families at levels 1–2; then 3–4; then 5–6. Add validators, variation, Undo and hints with each slice. | All six levels have real tasks; ambiguity/alternative-solution checks and browser QA pass |
| 2 — Chinese | Establish character rendering and reviewed vocabulary; add levels 1–2, then phrases/sentences, then original stories | Script pack consistent; narrator/reading modes clear; accepted language alternatives reviewed |
| 3 — English | Establish vocabulary/print tasks; then sentences; then comprehension. Test audio separately before any listening strand. | Silent core works; sound-dependent tasks never appear as independently playable without support |
| 4 — Science | Author sourced observation packs, then prediction/evidence tasks and fair-comparison reasoning | Factual and evidence consistency pass; prediction feedback remains neutral |
| 5 — Stories | Author reviewed scenes, then perspectives and branches, then ambiguity/tradeoffs | Every branch ends; multiple reasonable responses and boundaries are preserved |
| 6 — Whole app | Audit Hub navigation, difficulty descriptions, content density, storage fallback and documentation | Existing math/diagnostic checks still pass; subject pages verified live; device limits recorded |

For each subject, first author at least six canonical examples per level (two per initial family) with answers, distractor reasons and alternate accepted solutions before designing a random generator. Eighteen families per subject are a staged target; reduce visible launch scope honestly if editorial quality or device layout needs more work. Do not replace missing content with repetitive cosmetic variants.

## Verification and acceptance checklist

- Content review: meaningful pictures; one intended objective; no unintended reading requirement; factual/language review; answer rationale and alternate solutions included.
- Generator review: exercise at least 100 generated tasks per deterministic subject/level combination across several seeds; include constant-random fallback, finite banks and repeated letters. These are software samples, not a pedagogical study. Validate invariants with an independent oracle where feasible.
- Interaction checks: correct/incorrect attempt, hint before/after phase change, Undo, final completion, same-level continuation beyond three questions, menu return, cross-subject state isolation and disabled storage. Multi-answer/open stories must not be tested as single-answer quizzes.
- Structural difficulty: compare fixed examples at adjacent levels and document the new relationship/skill. If the only change is more cards/text, redesign it.
- Browser QA: render each template and a dense boundary example; check narrow layout, contrast, tap targets, cue placement and word/glyph wrapping. Inspect the deployed page rather than relying solely on jsdom.
- Kindle QA remains separate: exact model/firmware, Chinese glyphs, black-and-white asset legibility, scrolling, touch response, E Ink refresh and optional audio. Desktop passing is not Kindle passing.
- Observe use with the three-year-old: can the child tell where to act after one pictured demonstration; which words require narration; whether an error explains a rule without frustration. Record reusable findings, not a numerical ability diagnosis.
- Keep source/data/assets loaded for session interaction; distinguish a temporary disconnection from offline reload/reopening. No new capability claim without the relevant test.
- Update current subject documentation, handoff and status after each lasting decision. This plan replaces obsolete scope in place; no chronological change log.

## Evidence and design boundaries

The six-level sequence, specific task families and text budgets above are original product proposals. No cited framework validates these exact tasks, proves app effectiveness or assigns them to a particular age.

- [EEF — Preparing for Literacy](https://educationendowmentfoundation.org.uk/education-evidence/guidance-reports/literacy-early-years): early literacy includes communication, adult-child interaction, stories and sound activities. This supports preserving narration and meaning alongside print work; it does not validate a silent vocabulary app as a complete language curriculum.
- [Head Start — Science Preschool](https://headstart.gov/school-readiness/article/science-preschool): observation, comparison, predictions, investigations and interpreting results inform the science strands. The source describes preschool development; our levels 4–6 extend the product design and are not its age norms.
- [Head Start — Early Learning Outcomes Framework](https://www.headstart.gov/interactive-head-start-early-learning-outcomes-framework-ages-birth-five): distinguishes language/literacy, cognition and social-emotional development. Use this to keep subject objectives clear, not as a scorecard or evidence for the app's later levels.

Later authoring should attach item-specific sources for science facts and editorial review for Chinese/English. Audio, handwriting, speech recognition and a complete formal curriculum remain outside the first implementation scope.
